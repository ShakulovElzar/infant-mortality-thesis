# International child-mortality datasets beyond US CDC/NVSS

Checked 20 November 2026 against primary provider documentation. These sources are **alternatives or external checks**, not additional rows that can be appended to a US child's CDC birth–death record. Download byte sizes were not published on the accessible provider pages; do not claim a 1–3 GB figure for these datasets until an actual package is selected and measured. No large files were downloaded.

| Source | Unit and measured outcome | Fields / scale | Access and thesis fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| [DHS Program](https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/DHSG1/Guide_to_DHS_Statistics_DHS-8.pdf) birth recode (BR) | One row per live birth reported by a sampled woman; birth date, survival status and age at death support infant and under-5 mortality estimates. [DHS birth-history documentation](https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/MR11/MR11.pdf). | Women's and households' characteristics; birth order, sex, date, survival/age at death; more recent children may have pregnancy, delivery and child-health information. DHS has run [hundreds of surveys in >90 countries](https://api.dhsprogram.com/). India NFHS-5 alone interviewed [724,115 women in 636,699 households](https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/OF43/India_National_Fact_Sheet.pdf), but this is **not** the number of eligible births/deaths. | Free after registration and country-specific access permission. Strongest international individual-level comparison; select 2–4 countries/surveys with comparable fields and verify death counts/coverage. DHS API is **aggregate indicator data**, not these birth records. File sizes require approved download. |
| [UNICEF MICS](https://mics.ipums.org/mics/) / [IPUMS MICS](https://mics.ipums.org/mics/) birth histories | One record per reported live birth, with child's sex, birth date, survival and age at death. [MICS6 model questionnaire](https://mics.ipums.org/mics/resources/enum_materials_pdf/survey_form_mics6_wm.pdf). | Cross-country harmonized portal currently advertises **94 countries, 237 samples, >2,000 variables, 18 million person records**; this total includes many kinds of people, not 18m birth-death labels. The [Bangladesh MICS 2019 catalog](https://micsnada.unicef.org/index.php/catalog/447/data-dictionary) lists **121,184 birth-history cases, 50 variables**, alongside 68,709 women and 64,400 households; the [2025 Bangladesh birth-history table](https://micsnada.unicef.org/index.php/catalog/449/data-dictionary/F4?file_name=bh) lists **114,943 births, 54 variables**. | UNICEF access/research registration; IPUMS helps select harmonized variables but directs the user to UNICEF for delivery. Good separate country/time validation and under-5 analysis; exact ZIP sizes and permissions need verification after access. |
| [CHAMPS](https://champshealth.org/causes-of-death) | Deceased stillbirths/children <5 from surveillance sites, with expert-reviewed underlying, immediate and intermediate causes of death. **No comparable sample of all surviving children in the public curated case dataset.** | Provider page shows **8,947 completed cases** (live counter; date shown 26 March 2025); [methods](https://www.champshealth.org/champs-methodology) combine verbal autopsy, clinical records, laboratory PCR/pathology and maternal factors for perinatal deaths. | De-identified standard data after registration and electronic DTA; limited data require DUA and estimated 4–8 weeks. Excellent thesis on *cause of death among deaths* or diagnostic agreement; inappropriate as a training set for probability of death among births. Size not verified. |
| [INDEPTH iSHARE / HDSS](https://www.indepth-ishare.org/index.php/catalog/central/about) | Longitudinal surveillance episodes: births, deaths, migration and person-time at local sites. [INDEPTH PopStats](https://indepth-network.org/indepth-popstats/) exposes aggregated infant/child/under-5 rates. | Potentially stronger follow-up than a one-time household survey, but detailed pregnancy/maternal covariates and comparable site coverage vary. | Repository describes licensed/restricted access. The iSHARE catalog returned errors during this check; no current file/package size or specific accessible site sample confirmed. Exploratory option only, not March-critical path. |
| [UN IGME/CME Info](https://childmortality.org/) and [UNICEF child mortality](https://data.unicef.org/topic/child-survival/under-five-mortality/) | Country-year infant/neonatal/under-5 rates and deaths, **statistical estimates** rather than individual outcomes. | Broad global trends; site offers tables and downloads. UNICEF explains the estimates are smoothed/extrapolated from empirical series with uncertainty. | Open contextual benchmarking only. Do not attach country rates as if they label an individual US birth or claim they are measured patient outcomes. File size not verified. |
| [WHO Mortality Database](https://www.who.int/data/data-collection-tools/who-mortality-database) | Country-year-age-sex-ICD cause counts reported by national registration systems; not person-level. | Raw CSV can compare cause patterns where registration is sufficiently complete; WHO notes country coverage and coding differences. | Open aggregate external context; not a direct row-level augment to CDC US linked file. File size not verified. |

## Crucial comparability limits

1. **No personal join to US CDC:** these providers do not share a cross-dataset child identifier. The US public linked file also removes fine geography, so international or local exposures cannot be merged onto each child as if observed for that household.
2. **Different ascertainment:** US CDC links official birth and death certificates; DHS/MICS ask surviving/interviewed mothers to recall births and age at death. Sampling, recall, missed deceased mothers and geography change the target population.
3. **Time leakage and censoring:** maternal BMI/BP measured at a later DHS/NFHS interview cannot represent a prenatal measurement for an earlier child. Children born within a year of interview have not completed their first-year risk window; use survival methods or a completed-follow-up cohort. Pregnancy/child-care variables need survey- and birth-year availability checks.
4. **Interpretation of size:** 18 million IPUMS MICS *person records* cover mixed units; the relevant number is linked live births with complete observation and deaths under the chosen age. Select a package only after checking those counts and the actual download bytes.

## Recommended use for the thesis

Keep US CDC linked birth–infant death as the main dataset for one-year mortality. If broadening beyond the US is desired, use DHS BR or MICS birth histories as **separate external analyses** on common predictors (maternal age, birth order, plurality, sex, socioeconomic measures); do not pool all rows into one model without substantial harmonization. If the research question shifts from *risk of death* to *why deaths occur*, CHAMPS is the most interesting distinct source, but it answers a different question.
